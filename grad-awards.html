About the Awards

Welcome to the first-ever Sikh Graduate Awards

The Sikh Graduate Awards are a national celebration recognising the achievements, ambition and contribution of Sikh graduates from across the UK. These awards have been created to celebrate Sikh graduate success and to shine a light on those who have demonstrated academic excellence, professional promise, leadership, resilience, seva and a commitment to making a positive difference in society.

This event is more than an awards ceremony. It is an opportunity for Sikh graduates to connect with Sikh professionals, build confidence, hear from inspiring speakers, meet other ambitious graduates and celebrate success with family members, community leaders, universities and employers.

The Sikh Graduate Awards are open to Sikh graduates who have completed a recognised higher education qualification, including an undergraduate degree, postgraduate qualification, PhD or Level 6 apprenticeship. Nominees should have graduated within the last two years or be in the early stages of their professional journey following completion of their qualification.

Why Attend?

Designed for graduates ready to take the next step — connecting, learning, being recognised and building a professional identity grounded in Sikh values.

Who Can Be Nominated?

Open to Sikh graduates across the UK

To be eligible, nominees must meet the following criteria:

  • Be Sikh
  • Have completed a recognised higher education qualification — including an undergraduate degree, postgraduate qualification, PhD or Level 6 apprenticeship — during 2025 or 2026
  • Be able to demonstrate achievement, growth, impact, service, leadership or resilience during their education or early professional journey

Whether you have achieved outstanding academic results, overcome personal barriers, served your community, started a business, led a student society, supported others or shown exceptional future potential, we want to hear your story.

Graduates can nominate themselves or be nominated by someone else — including a family member, lecturer, employer, mentor, university, community organisation or Sikh society.
